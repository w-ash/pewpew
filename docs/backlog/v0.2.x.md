Phase 1). A pure render core with the ADR-005 authoring contract, the §8 dev
loop (preview sink → hot reload → golden frames), one paced clock (ADR-010),
and DDP 8-bit out to a stock WLED node (ADR-003/004). Everything later builds
on this; nothing here is throwaway.

#### Authoring surface

Shared context: ADR-005 (Accepted 2026-07-26) is the contract —
`before_render(dt, features)` + `render(x, y, z, index)` over normalized 0–1
coordinate **arrays**, persistent `self.pixels`, time-parameterized motion,
per-frame exception containment. `bench/authoring/surface.py` is the
throwaway prototype of it; the five patterns in `bench/authoring/` are the
acceptance corpus — they must port to the real engine with their behavior
unchanged. Stumble numbers refer to `bench/results/authoring-2026-07-16.md`.

- [x] **Pure render core + pattern contract**
    - Effort: L
    - What: `render(t, mapping, params, seed) → frame` as a pure function, with the ADR-005 pattern base class, normalized world coordinates, persistent frame buffer, and per-frame exception containment.
    - Why: Every other story in this series and every later phase sits on it; purity is what makes golden frames, deterministic scrubbing, and the fleet twin possible (§8, T-1).
    - Dependencies: v0.1.x §12 items 1–2
    - Status: Completed (2026-07-27)
    - Notes: `src/pewpew/{pattern,kit,render,mapping,features}.py`; supersedes `bench/authoring/surface.py`. All five patterns ported to `src/pewpew/patterns/` and proven **bit-identical** (`np.array_equal`, not RMS-tolerant) against the prototype over the study's own 120 frames at seed 42 — `tests/test_port_equivalence.py`. Purity lands at the *sequence* level, not the frame level: ADR-005 patterns are deliberately stateful, so `render_sequence(factory, mapping, params, seed, frames, fps)` is the pure function §8 means, and `Renderer.advance_to(t)`/`.step(dt)` is the live form the paced clock will drive. Timestamps accumulate `t += 1.0/fps` to match `FakeClock` bit-for-bit without importing it (engine must not depend on the harness). `src/harness/` untouched. Also landed: the `time()` module-global hack is gone (frame-scoped `ContextVar`), and a `params` key naming an undeclared `Param` now raises rather than creating a dead knob.

- [x] **Idiom-kit additions mandated by the §12 item-2 verdict**
    - Effort: M
    - What: Add `fade(dt, tau)`, `bins(x, n)`, `sample2d(field, x, y)`, and `hash01(x, salt)` to the standard idiom kit alongside the ADR-005 built-ins (`time()`, `wave()`, `triangle()`, `square()`).
    - Why: The authoring-surface GO was *conditional* on these four — they shield the stumbles the study measured (threshold/dtype edges, frame-rate-dependent decay, per-pixel hashing).
    - Dependencies: Pure render core
    - Status: Completed (2026-07-27)
    - Notes: In `src/pewpew/kit.py`, each a bit-exact substitute for the study expression it replaces, with `tests/test_kit.py` asserting that against the inlined original. Shipped as `hash01(x, seed, salt)` — **seed and salt stay two arguments, deliberately**: the study's `12.9898*x64 + self.seed + salt` associates left-to-right, and a pre-summed salt diverges by 1 ulp on 46 of the study's 120 frames. That is stumble #6's own failure mode one step upstream, so the signature reproduces the study by construction rather than by luck. `fade` is dt-based per Q-5. Adopted at every call site except fire's `lift`, which is `exp(-dt*rise)`, not `exp(-dt/tau)`.

- [x] **Authoring docs for the three measured friction points**
    - Effort: S
    - What: Document the internal-field idiom, that `np.where` evaluates both branches (so guards must be safe, not lazy), and where RNG belongs so patterns stay seed-reproducible.
    - Why: 13 of the study's 17 stumbles were expertise-shielded — an author without NumPy fluency hits them. Docs are the cheapest mitigation the study identified.
    - Dependencies: Idiom-kit additions
    - Status: Completed (2026-07-27)
    - Notes: `docs/authoring.md`. Covers the mandated three (#12/#15, #5, #14) plus the three the study's own "Reading of the evidence" clusters with them: private per-pixel state (#17), dt-decay (#2), and threshold/dtype edges (#6, #9). Every claim cites its `# STUMBLE-N` anchor in `bench/authoring/*.py`, and every example was executed against the real engine rather than written from the docstrings.

- [x] **Decide and implement private per-pixel state buffers (stumble #17)**
    - Effort: M
    - What: Decide whether patterns may declare private per-pixel arrays alongside the sanctioned `self.pixels`, and implement the outcome (including lifecycle across hot reload).
    - Why: The study left this open; fire and any particle pattern need per-pixel state beyond the output buffer, so leaving it undecided blocks a faithful port.
    - Dependencies: Pure render core
    - Status: Completed (2026-07-27)
    - Notes: **Decided yes**, recorded as `## Amendment (2026-07-27)` in ADR-005 with architecture §3 following it. A `setup(n, x, y, z, index)` hook hands the pattern its coordinates once per binding; `self.buffer(name, shape)` declared there is engine-managed (zeroed on rebind, reallocated on mapping change, read-only inside `render`). **Coords-at-setup is the actual unlock** — #17's bind was that `before_render` never sees coordinates. Precedent converged across three lineages: ISF's manifest-declared host-allocated `PERSISTENT` buffers [S218], Shadertoy's previous-state/next-state buffers [S219], and Pixelblaze's persistent arrays [S680] — the last of which pays for them with an *impure render*, the trade we explicitly reject. Registry keys on name+spec, never array identity, because authors rebind (fire does). Two follow-ons this leaves open, both for the hot-reload story: `setup()` failures are deliberately *not* contained (they propagate out of `Renderer.__init__`, so the reload supervisor must keep the last-good binding itself), and scalar per-pattern state (`self.phase`, `self.head`) is not engine-tracked, so reload must decide whether it carries. Also worth a later pass: comet's sparkle still uses the study's time-salted workaround rather than a private buffer, since "behavior unchanged" was the port's acceptance bar — so the corpus does not yet exercise the feature #17 motivated (`docs/authoring.md` §2 demonstrates it instead). Unblocks "Port the upgraded fire to Python" in `unscheduled.md`.

- [x] **Typed params (§5) + param-liveness check in the L1 harness**
    - Effort: M
    - What: Typed `Param` declarations per §5/ADR-006, plus a harness check that wiggles each declared param across its range and asserts the frame sequence actually changes.
    - Why: The sdf3d `spin` parameter was a mathematical no-op and *nothing* caught it — not the authoring surface, not golden frames, because other motion masked it.
    - Dependencies: Pure render core
    - Status: Completed (2026-08-22)
    - Notes: `Param` now validates its own declaration (`lo < hi`, default in range), learns its `name`/`owner` via `__set_name__`, and derives `step`/`decimals`; `declared_params(cls)` reads declarations back off the class and `ParamSet` holds them alongside current values (`src/pewpew/pattern.py`, exported from `pewpew`). Governing record is `## Amendment (2026-08-22)` in ADR-006 — status stays *Proposed*, since Phase 1 lands only the declaration half and the canonical tree (paths, partial-merge, presets, `save`, mDNS) is Phase 3. The Phase-1 object is deliberately a **`ParamSet`, not a tree**: no paths, no nesting, and it must not pre-spend the vocabulary the tree will need. **Range decision: refuse, not clamp** — a clamp renders one value while every surface displays another, which is the dead-knob failure one layer up; float noise at an endpoint snaps (a slider stepping `lo + i*step` can land an ulp past `hi`), anything further out is refused. Refusals split into `reason` (network-safe) and `hint` (names the source file), because the preview server is unauthenticated by design and a refusal must not serve the layout of the disk. **The correction that mattered most**: the first plan resolved params per frame in `advance_to`, justified as preventing a tear between `before_render` and `render` (plasma reads `speed` in one, `scale` in the other). Wrong — `pace.py:130-137` drains `call_soon` at the top of the tick, *before* `advance_to`, so the tear is already impossible; and ADR-006's real per-frame seat must evaluate LFOs *at t*, which needs `t` and the feature frame, neither of which `apply(pattern)` has. Building it would have been the wrong signature for the right reason. Values resolve at bind and **the frame path is untouched**. **Second correction, a live crash path**: `call_soon` propagates a closure's exception out of `run()` (`pace.py:110-112`), so a raising `set_param` reached from the websocket would let a browser stop the show with one out-of-range slider — verified, `run()` dies with the ValueError. Hence `ParamSet.check()`: non-raising, and the single source of every refusal wording so a socket reply and `set`'s exception can never diverge. The check is `tests/test_param_liveness.py` — nine cases, one per (pattern, knob), self-extending as patterns gain knobs, endpoints rather than a nudge so the declared range is under test too. Acceptance measured both ways: with the xfail removed `sdf3d.spin` fails at **rms 2.65e-08** (not 0 — `angle` still accumulates into cos/sin, it just cannot move a y-axis-invariant SDF), and a freshly-added unread `Param` on geq fails at exactly 0.0. Bar is `LIVENESS_RMS = 1e-3` in `harness/goldens.py`, now shared with `test_golden_frames.py::test_seed_changes_output`, which had the same literal inline. **sdf3d stays frozen** — `test_port_equivalence.py` holds it bit-identical to the study prototype, so fixing the axis would break the evidence chain to repair the very bug that proves the check works; `xfail(strict=True)` means the exemption fails as XPASS if `spin` ever comes alive. Also landed: `Renderer.params` is the set (identity stable across `rebind`, since v0.2.3 holds a reference to it), `Renderer.set_param`, CLI validation moved *ahead* of `Renderer(...)` so a knob typo exits with one clean line while a bad mapping or a raising `setup` still gives a full traceback (binding is deliberately loud, `render.py:152-156` — a blanket `except ValueError` would have quietened it), and one canonical `STUDY_CASES` table in `pewpew.patterns` replacing the (class, mapping) triple three test files each re-derived by hand. Semantic change worth knowing: a `Param` declaration now beats an `__init__` assignment to the same name — the prototype's isinstance guard let `__init__` win — no corpus pattern does it, so bit-exactness holds. **Rejected with numbers**: a descriptor `Param` is the idiomatic 2026 answer and would type `self.speed` as `float`, but measured 4.15 ns plain / 7.4 ns for the current shape / 25–35 ns data descriptor, and the repo runs no mypy, pyright, CI or lint — zero benefit today; revisit when a type checker lands. **Two things v0.2.3 should not rediscover**: `SinkDriver` is *not* reusable for param coalescing (its newest-wins mailbox is one slot, so a `scale` set would discard a pending `speed` set — param coalescing is last-write-wins *per key*), and no rate limiting is needed (a drag fires 60–120 events/s against a 60 Hz drain, ~2 closures per tick); `describe()` is the hello payload, a list so display order survives transport. Left open: a live set only reaches a pattern that reads the knob per frame, so one precomputing from a knob in `setup` would ignore it — that wants a live-set liveness check when a pattern makes it real. This **arms** the second of ADR-010's three GC-retest triggers; the retest is a measurement, due once hot reload lands too.

#### Frame pipeline & sinks

- [x] **Paced render clock, deadline-corrected (ADR-010)**
    - Effort: M
    - What: One paced clock measuring elapsed, sleeping the remainder, and correcting accumulated error; per-sink newest-wins backpressure bounded at ~2 frames in flight.
    - Why: ADR-010's core mechanism; the single clock is also where modulated params and audio features resolve exactly once per frame.
    - Dependencies: Pure render core
    - Status: Completed (2026-08-20)
    - Notes: `src/pewpew/pace.py` — `PacedLoop` is the bench `deadline` pacer ported exactly (absolute schedule seeded once, skip-ahead with the no-`+1` rule and its rationale comment carried over), plus the spin variant as a `spin_margin` option with `measure_spin_margin()` for the host probe; the engine never auto-probes. The open timestamp question got decided and recorded as `## Amendment (2026-08-20)` in ADR-010: **engine time is nominal schedule time, never measured wall time** — `t += 1/fps` per tick by repeated addition, one further period per skipped slot — so a live show is bit-identical to `render_sequence` at the same slot (`tests/test_pace.py::test_engine_time_matches_render_sequence_bitwise`, `np.array_equal`). Working the skip arithmetic through fixed two wrong intuitions worth keeping: a 2.5-period stall drops exactly *one* slot (the next frame renders immediately, late in the last past slot, rather than idling), and a sub-2-period stall drops *zero* — both are what the no-`+1` rule buys. Backpressure ships as `SinkDriver` (per-sink worker + 1-slot mailbox = Fadecandy's 2-in-flight bound); `PacedLoop` itself takes plain `on_frame(t, frame)` callables handed one shared read-only copy per tick, so the Sink protocol stays owned by the sink story, which wraps its `send` in a `SinkDriver`. Seams left deliberately: `call_soon(fn)` is the loop-thread mutation point hot reload will use, and a callback exception (including an uncaught bind failure) propagates out of `run()` — the reload supervisor owns catching it. Features already resolve once per frame inside `advance_to`; the param tree will hook the same place (typed-params story). GC posture: one frame copy + one tuple per tick, no growing containers — the ADR-010 GC retest obligation (re-bench once sinks + param tree + hot reload exist) stands unchanged.

Runner: `python -m pewpew run <pattern> [--map] [--ansi] [--preview]` — minimal, hot reload extends it. - [x] **Live param controls on the preview page**
    - Effort: M
    - What: Sliders on the served preview page, one per declared `Param`, that set the running pattern's parameter values over the existing websocket while the show plays.
    - Why: The retired playground's best feature was knob-turning, but its sliders drove JS imitations; these drive the real engine, so what a knob shows is true (the dead-`spin` lesson). Restores the interactive half of the dev loop until Phase 3's full control plane.

    - Status: Completed (2026-08-27)
    - Notes: `Controls` is a two-method Protocol in `sinks/base.py` (`describe()`, `set(name, value) -> reason | None`), so `PreviewSink` imports neither `Renderer` nor `PacedLoop`; the runner's `_LoopControls` is the bridge. Wire protocol grew three shapes, all recorded in the `preview.py` module docstring: hello carries `params` (`ParamSet.describe()`, or `[]` when nothing is wired), `{"type":"set"}` is the only inbound message, `{"type":"refused"}` goes back to whoever asked, and `{"type":"params"}` goes to everyone. **The refusal carries `check`'s reason and never `hint`** — the hint names a source file and this port is unauthenticated by design — and `test_refused_set_names_the_range_but_never_a_source_path` asserts exactly that rather than trusting it. The sink writes no validation of its own: bools, non-numbers and non-finite values all arrive from `json.loads` and are `check`'s job, which keeps one wording for a refusal whether it came from a socket or the CLI. **The echo is recomposed per frame, not pushed when a set is accepted** — the first design pushed at accept time and would have shipped stale values, because a set is *queued* onto the loop thread and has not landed yet when `controls.set` returns. Rebuilding from `describe()` in `send` costs a measured 2.0 us on a SinkDriver worker, never touches the render loop, and is unconditionally true; clients see it only on a change, via the same current-state-not-delta trick that already makes `status` immune to newest-wins coalescing. No lock was needed for the handler thread's refusal reply racing a driver worker's frame: `websockets.sync`'s `Connection.send` serializes unfragmented sends on `protocol_mutex` (checked in `send_context`, not assumed). Both v0.2.2 warnings were honored — no `SinkDriver` for coalescing, no rate limiting. **A latent flake this surfaced and fixed**: `start()` returns as soon as the server thread is spawned, so a fast `stop()` closed the listener before `serve_forever` finished starting and it raised EBADF from its own opening log line. Harmless but noisy, and it went from 0-in-5 runs to ~25% once the six new socket tests changed the timing; `_serve` now tolerates an OSError while `_stopping` is set and nothing else. Verified 0 in 200 back-to-back start/stops. On the page: the lever row reuses the retired playground's CSS, a slider under the pointer is exempt from echoes so an update cannot fight a drag, and a refusal borrows the status bar for 4 s and hands it back rather than erasing a renderer error that is still true. **The screenshot caught what the statistics could not**: `#meta` was `position: fixed` at the viewport bottom, which is where the lever row now lives, so the readout overprinted the first slider — the footer is one flex row in flow now. Visual verification per `.claude/rules/preview-visual-verify.md` did both halves: glow bands stayed spread (low 48676 / mid 322548 / high 163951, so the b68b96a gamma is intact), `speed=0` froze the canvas to a bit-identical digest a second apart and `speed=2` moved it again (the unambiguous proof a slider reaches the engine), four `scale` values gave four distinct frozen digests, and a second tab followed the first. One metric that did *not* work, worth not repeating: band-crossing count along a scanline fell as `scale` rose, because 300 pixels alias badly past Nyquist — the frozen-digest comparison is the honest measure. Leaf-module re-import as §8 asks: each load execs a **fresh module object under a stable `sys.modules` key** (`pewpew_user.<stem>`), never `importlib.reload`, which re-execs into the same object so a renamed class leaves both names behind and, for a gallery file, would rebind `pewpew.patterns.comet.Comet` under `STUDY_CASES`. `swap` has a **strong exception guarantee**: the candidate binds against the inherited param values *before* anything on the renderer changes, so a raising `setup` propagates (bind stays loud, `render.py`) with the old pattern untouched — that settles open point (1). Open point (2) settled by user decision: plain scalar state (`self.head`, `self.phase`) is kept when the new instance has the same name at the same exact type after its own `__init__` (bool/int/float/str only; author-managed arrays start fresh — ADR-005's answer is `self.buffer`), and a name declared as a `Param` on either class is never treated as a scalar, so a knob demoted to a constant takes the author's new constant. Inheritance is a lifecycle hook, not an after-the-fact copy: `Inheritance(old)` (`pattern.py`) is handed to `_bind`, applies `__init__` scalars and the RNG state *before* `setup` (so what `setup` assigns is the new code's own), and each `self.buffer(...)` declaration takes over the old array when name and full `BufferSpec` match (`pixels` included), so a view `setup` derives from a buffer refers to the kept data. #### First installation (show 2026-11-13 to 16)

Shared context (added 2026-10-19). Pewpew's first real deployment, in one
hotel room: build check-in 2026-11-10, show 2026-11-13 to 16.

- **Rig.** A laptop renders and sends DDP through a private travel router to
  3 ESP32-S3 DevKitC-1 boards running stock WLED v16.0.1, each behind a
  74AHCT125 level shifter. The fixtures are salvaged: up to 60 RGBW pixel
  bars (1 m, 64 px, SK6812-type, 24 V, 4 bytes/px) and possibly 12 RGB tubes
  (1.5 m, 36 px, UCS1903, 3 bytes/px). Proposed layout (2026-10-19, not
  yet confirmed): 20 bars per board, 5 per output, on the S3's 4 RMT
  outputs. Open hardware questions (connector pin map,
  RGBW channel order, tube data rate, measured watts per bar) are tracked
  outside this repo.
- **Arithmetic.** 60 × 64 = 3,840 bar pixels, 1,280 per board. One 5-bar
  output takes 320 × 40 µs = 12.8 ms, so the LED wire caps near 76 fps. At
  40 fps RGB each board receives about 154 KB/s. Render cost is not a
  concern: the Phase-0 NumPy kernels took 0.03–0.08 ms per frame at 3,000 px
  (`bench/results/throughput-2026-07-15-mac-m5pro.json`).
- **WLED facts, read from the v16.0.1 source.**
    - DDP pixel format is per packet: 4 bytes when data-type bits 3–5 are
      `011`, else 3; `channelOffset` counts bytes (`wled00/e131.cpp:54-57`).
    - RGB data on an RGBW output gets W from the output's auto-white mode,
      streamed data included (`wled00/bus_manager.cpp:275`; `autoWhiteCalc`
      at `:102-125`). "Accurate" moves the shared part of R, G and B into W.
    - A new streamed frame is shown only when more than 15 ms have passed
      since the last show (`wled00/udp.cpp:477`): about 66 fps at most.
    - Per-board limits: 16,384 px per board and 2,048 per output
      (`wled00/const.h` at the research pin `14709dd`); the S3 has 4 RMT and
      8 parallel outputs.
    - After the realtime timeout (2,500 ms default) a board returns to its own
      state, so its boot preset is the fallback look.
- **Wi-Fi is the main risk.** No measured report was found of stock WLED on
  an ESP32-S3 at this pixel count. Bandwidth is small next to the S3's
  30 Mbit/s UDP receive (Espressif Wi-Fi performance guide); airtime
  contention in a crowded hotel is the threat. A WLED developer puts
  live-mode stutter down to the Wi-Fi signal
  ([wled/WLED#5596](https://github.com/wled/WLED/issues/5596)). Stock WLED
  has no Ethernet on the S3
  ([wled/WLED#5478](https://github.com/wled/WLED/issues/5478)).
- **Trust.** The control page and event inputs are unauthenticated by
  design (architecture §5 trust model), which holds only because the router
  is private. Bind to the router's interface, never to hotel Wi-Fi.
- **Hardware tiers (CLAUDE.md T-7).** Before the show the boards are bench
  hardware (tier 1): flashing and config writes go `--dry-run` first and are
  audit-logged. During the show they are deployed (tier 2): agents only
  read, and health polling is read-only.
